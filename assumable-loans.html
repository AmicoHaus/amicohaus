Assumable government-backed loans (VA, FHA and USDA): how they work

Some home loans can be taken over by the next buyer, rate and all. Here's how that works, which loans qualify, and who handles each step.

Any qualified buyer may apply to assume. You don't need to be a veteran to take over a VA loan. Every assumption needs approval from the loan servicer (and VA, HUD or USDA where required) and buyer qualification.

What "assuming a loan" means

When you assume a loan, you take over the seller's existing mortgage (its balance, interest rate and remaining term) instead of getting a brand-new loan. If the seller's rate is lower than today's rates, that can mean a lower monthly payment for the buyer.

You still buy the home at an agreed price. The difference between the price and the loan balance (the seller's equity) is paid in cash at closing or with a separate second loan from a licensed lender.

How a VA loan assumption works

  1. Anyone who qualifies can apply. The buyer applies with the seller's loan servicer, which checks credit and income much like any loan. VA's target is to finish within 45 days, and a denial can be appealed.
  2. The equity gap. The buyer covers the difference between the price and the loan balance in cash or with a second loan through escrow.
  3. The VA funding fee. A 0.5% VA funding fee applies unless the buyer is exempt, paid at closing.
  4. Release of liability. With servicer approval, the seller is released from the loan.
  5. The seller's VA entitlement. It stays with the loan until it's paid off, unless a qualified veteran buyer substitutes their own entitlement. Sellers who plan to use a VA loan again should ask their servicer how this affects them.

VA limits what the buyer can be charged in an assumption to a short list (things like the servicer's processing fee, the funding fee, credit report, recording, title, taxes and insurance). Amico Haus never charges buyers anything.

Which loans can be assumed?

Loan typeAssumable?
VAYes, with servicer approval (loans from before March 1, 1988 are freely assumable)
FHAYes, with approval, and the buyer must live in the home (loans from before December 1986 are freely assumable)
USDAYes, with approval; the buyer must meet income limits and live in the home
Conventional, fixed rateGenerally no
Conventional, adjustable rateSometimes. Ask the servicer

Not sure what kind of loan a home has? The seller's mortgage statement or servicer can confirm it.

Two owners, two assumable loans: a trade is two sales

If two owners both have assumable loans, each can buy the other's home and assume the other's loan. That's two separate, fully approved sales that close at the same time: each servicer approves its new buyer, and each sale has its own escrow and title.

  • Each home is reassessed for property tax at its sale price. Prop 19 may let owners 55 and over, severely disabled owners or wildfire/disaster victims who move to a new primary home within two years keep their tax base; check with the County Assessor.
  • Each sale has its own transfer tax, escrow and title costs.
  • Each owner's sale is taxed on its own (the home-sale exclusion may cover it). It isn't a 1031 exchange.
  • Any difference in equity is settled through escrow, in cash or with a second loan.

Thinking about renting each other's homes instead? That raises separate loan, landlord-tenant, insurance and tax questions, so talk to a real estate attorney before agreeing to anything.

Who does what

  • Amico Haus explains how this works and connects you with a licensed agent. It isn't a lender, doesn't take loan applications or approve loans, and no money moves through it.
  • The loan servicer (and VA, HUD or USDA where required) approves the buyer and the assumption.
  • A licensed loan officer handles any second loan for the equity gap.
  • Your real estate agent handles the purchase contract.
  • Escrow and title handle all funds and recording.
